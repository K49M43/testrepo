## Decision and consequence

1. A direct sovereign holding requires a specified security or explicit rolling policy, issue/coupon/maturity terms, dated purchase/sale prices (or a clearly bounded alternative mark), accrued interest, coupon reinvestment, fees, taxes, and a 2036 exit/rollover rule. A bond maturing before 2036 cannot be treated as a terminal 2036 holding without modeling its proceeds and reinvestment.
2. RBI monthly yield curves can inform a transparent constant-maturity or repricing scenario, but that scenario must not be labeled a historical direct-bond total return or executable portfolio result.
3. CASBI may reduce the market-history gap for a **sovereign index proxy** if its full data history and permitted use can be confirmed. It does not close direct-bond execution, tax, or cashflow gates.
4. CCIL trade-by-trade history offers the most security-specific route found, but requires sign-in and its commercial/redistribution terms are material. No attempt to access restricted pages or submit a third-party request was made.

**Current status:** the route review improves source understanding but acquires no price or index observations. The direct-GoI path, CCIL historical data rights, and sovereign proxy histories remain WP1 blockers. Do not calculate sovereign strategy performance, cross-asset correlation, or investment probability from these leads. Investment status remains **NO-GO / NOT YET EVALUATED**.

## Bounded next steps

- In parallel, assess publicly published CASBI/T-bill index files and use terms. If values are available only under a CCIL undertaking/license, obtain investor authorization before making a formal request; no access or permission is implied.
- Keep the direct GoI strategy separate: define an investable security/rollover policy only after its acquisition and 2036 exit path are evidenced.
- Accept an official data series only after raw-source retention, date/identity checks, independent anchors, and methodology review.
