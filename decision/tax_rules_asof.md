# Indian investment tax treatment map

**As of:** 3 November 2026

**Scope:** Research map for a resident individual investing in listed Indian instruments through a non-business personal account. This is an implementation specification, not individual tax advice. Residency, chosen tax regime, age, total income, security identity, transaction route, and later law can change the result.

**Tax year in force:** TY 2026–27. Tax years beginning before 1 April 2026 remain governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 under the savings provisions.

## Rules the model must encode

0. Income-tax Act, 2025 section 516 requires total income and amounts payable/refundable to ignore paise and round to the nearest ₹10, with ₹5 or more in the remaining rupee amount rounded upward. An isolated Decimal helper now implements and audits this primitive. It is not yet applied to the component tax calculation: the order of rounding total income relative to the mixed ordinary/special-income calculation and threshold composition must be documented and independently checked before integration. See the [official Act, section 516](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/Documents/Act/Income-tax-Act-2025.pdf).

1. Use the Income-tax Act, 2025 for tax years from 1 April 2026. Its section 202 is the default regime for eligible individuals, with slab rates of 0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30% across bands from ₹0–4L, ₹4–8L, ₹8–12L, ₹12–16L, ₹16–20L, ₹20–24L, and above ₹24L. The Finance Act 2026 provides the TY 2026–27 rates, 4% health and education cess, and applicable surcharge/marginal-relief rules. The resident-individual ₹60,000 rebate under section 156 has eligibility limits and must not be applied to special-rate capital gains. An investor’s stated “30% slab” is a marginal-rate input for ordinary income, not a universal tax rate.
2. Keep ordinary income and capital-gains calculations separate, then combine them as required for taxpayer-level thresholds, surcharge, marginal relief, and cess. Finance Act 2026 section 3(4)(a)(ii) excludes section 202 taxpayers from the general surcharge rule in section 3(4)(a) for tax computed under sections 196, 197, and 198. It does **not** exempt them from surcharge altogether: section 3(4)(b), Table row 10, separately applies the section 202 surcharge schedule. That schedule uses total-income bands and income composition; it includes specified gains in the threshold tests, caps surcharge attributable to tax on sections 196/197/198 income at 15%, and caps the section 202 surcharge at 25% rather than 37%. Apply the statutory marginal-relief rules and then 4% cess on tax plus surcharge. Never model section 202 special-rate capital gains as automatically surcharge-free.
3. Preserve acquisition lots and realization dates. Section 76 overrides ordinary holding-period classification for specified mutual-fund units acquired on/after 1 April 2023. Do not apply one “debt rate” to direct bonds and debt funds.
4. Treat all tax rates as current-law scenarios only. Re-evaluate enacted law, rules, circulars, and the investor’s tax profile before any 2036 exit.

## Tax-year and government-security exception notes

- The Income-tax Act, 2025 commenced on 1 April 2026. Section 536 preserves the 1961 Act for pre-1-April-2026 tax years and related proceedings. A long simulation spanning 2026–2036 therefore needs year-specific statutory regimes and transition rules, not one timeless rate table.
- The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 added Schedule IV entries 13D and 13E for specified Government-security interest and capital gains of a Foreign Institutional Investor and the Bank for International Settlements. CBDT Notification 137/2026 (G.S.R. 901(E), 19 October 2026) notified Rule 281A and Form 173A for those claims. These provisions are in force by this as-of date but are limited to the listed institutional persons; they do not create a general resident retail exemption. Do not assume eligibility without verifying taxpayer status and prescribed reporting.
- For TY 2026–27, section 202 taxpayers have a separate surcharge treatment in Finance Act 2026 section 3(4)(b), Table row 10. In contrast to the earlier erroneous reading, section 3(4)(a)(ii) only excludes these taxpayers from the general surcharge clause in section 3(4)(a); it does not eliminate surcharge on their special-rate gains. Under Table row 10, thresholds include capital gains under sections 196/197/198; surcharge on the tax attributable to that specified gain is capped at 15%, while the other section 202 surcharge tiers, income-composition rules, and marginal relief still matter. The 4% cess applies to income tax plus surcharge under section 3(15). For calculation, follow the enacted section 3 and its First Schedule rather than applying a flat surcharge percentage to the portfolio.
- Arithmetic boundary examples for the section 202 schedule, surcharge caps, marginal relief, and cess are recorded in `decision/tax_surcharge_examples_ty2026-27.md`. The examples are review material, not professional tax validation.

## Required after-tax engine behavior

- Maintain a ledger by instrument, acquisition date, units, acquisition cost, transaction charges, distributions/coupons, and sale/redemption date.
- At each tax-year boundary, calculate each lot’s holding period and legal classification, aggregate eligible equity LTCG against the ₹1.25L allowance, calculate ordinary-income slabs/rebate/surcharge/marginal relief, and apply cess. Avoid treating withholding as final tax.
- Capital losses require a separate, versioned loss ledger. Section 108 permits same-year short-term loss to offset income from any other capital asset and long-term loss to offset only other long-term capital gains. Under section 111, carried-forward short-term loss can offset capital gains of any character, while long-term loss remains restricted to long-term gains; the maximum period is eight succeeding tax years. Section 121 requires the loss to be determined pursuant to a return filed under section 263(1). Section 536 preserves eligible pre-1-April-2026 losses under the corresponding old-Act treatment. Rate-bucket allocation remains unresolved for this component model; see `decision/capital_loss_and_marginal_relief_review.md`. The current fixture ledger does not implement those offsets or carry-forwards and must fail closed on a realized loss.
- Section 516 requires total income and any amount payable/refundable to be rounded to the nearest ₹10 after ignoring paise. The current component calculator omits statutory rounding; do not treat its fractional-rupee outputs as a final tax payable/refund. Before integrating rounding, specify how rounded total income interacts with normal-rate and special-rate components and validate worked cases.
- Compare at least these exit cases: liquidate all lots in the target year; liquidate in tranches across tax years; hold a direct bond to contractual maturity while receiving coupons; and the applicable debt-fund redemption case. Do not model redemption proceeds and a coupon as the same cashflow.
- Reconcile terminal nominal balance to tax payable, fees and trading friction, then deflate using the selected observed CPI anchor and scenario paths. Keep CPI uncertainty separate from tax-law uncertainty.
- Before final use, validate against a qualified Indian tax professional’s worked examples for resident status, regime election, dividend/coupon timing, section 76 and Schedule IV eligibility.

## Current implementation checkpoint

`ty2026_27_tax_components.py` implements a bounded resident-individual research component for section 202 slabs, section 156 rebate, eligible sections 196/197/198 gains, section 76 gain arithmetic after an explicit eligibility confirmation, the section 202 surcharge schedule and special-income cap, ordinary-only surcharge marginal relief, cess, and withholding credits. `audit_ty2026_27_tax_components.py` reproduces the worked examples and tested boundaries. Amounts use `Decimal`; tax-return rupee rounding is omitted.

The component deliberately fails closed for unconfirmed special-rate eligibility, section 76 without both predicates, losses/set-offs, basic-exemption allocation across multiple special-gain sections, and mixed-income surcharge marginal relief where the statutory threshold composition is material. It is not a complete taxpayer-year computation or event/lot ledger. A qualified tax professional has not reviewed the examples, and security-level tax classification, investor income, actual costs, and later law remain unqualified. Section 516 has an isolated audited helper but is not integrated. No investor-specific after-tax result is qualified. **Investment authorization remains NO-GO.**
