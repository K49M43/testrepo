# Indian investment tax treatment map

**As of:** 2 November 2026

**Scope:** Research map for a resident individual investing in listed Indian instruments through a non-business personal account. This is an implementation specification, not individual tax advice. Residency, chosen tax regime, age, total income, security identity, transaction route, and later law can change the result.

**Tax year in force:** TY 2026–27. Tax years beginning before 1 April 2026 remain governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 under the savings provisions.

## Rules the model must encode

1. Use the Income-tax Act, 2025 for tax years from 1 April 2026. Its section 202 is the default regime for eligible individuals, with slab rates of 0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30% across bands from ₹0–4L, ₹4–8L, ₹8–12L, ₹12–16L, ₹16–20L, ₹20–24L, and above ₹24L. The Finance Act 2026 provides the TY 2026–27 rates, 4% health and education cess, and applicable surcharge/marginal-relief rules. The resident-individual ₹60,000 rebate under section 156 has eligibility limits and must not be applied to special-rate capital gains. An investor’s stated “30% slab” is a marginal-rate input for ordinary income, not a universal tax rate.
2. Keep ordinary income and capital-gains calculations separate. Apply basic exemption, section 156 rebate, surcharge, marginal relief, and cess at the taxpayer level after combining all relevant income. Special capital-gains schedules have their own conditions. In particular, for a person taxed under section 202, Finance Act 2026 section 3(4)(a)(ii) excludes surcharge on tax calculated under sections 196, 197, and 198; cess still applies.
3. Preserve acquisition lots and realization dates. Section 76 overrides ordinary holding-period classification for specified mutual-fund units acquired on/after 1 April 2023. Do not apply one “debt rate” to direct bonds and debt funds.
4. Treat all tax rates as current-law scenarios only. Re-evaluate enacted law, rules, circulars, and the investor’s tax profile before any 2036 exit.

## Tax-year and government-security exception notes

- The Income-tax Act, 2025 commenced on 1 April 2026. Section 536 preserves the 1961 Act for pre-1-April-2026 tax years and related proceedings. A long simulation spanning 2026–2036 therefore needs year-specific statutory regimes and transition rules, not one timeless rate table.
- The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 added Schedule IV entries 13D and 13E for specified Government-security interest and capital gains of a Foreign Institutional Investor and the Bank for International Settlements. CBDT Notification 137/2026 (G.S.R. 901(E), 19 October 2026) notified Rule 281A and Form 173A for those claims. These provisions are in force by this as-of date but are limited to the listed institutional persons; they do not create a general resident retail exemption. Do not assume eligibility without verifying taxpayer status and prescribed reporting.
- Section 202 slab tax uses the tax year’s total income. Surcharge starts only at the relevant total-income thresholds and marginal relief may apply. Finance Act 2026 section 3(4)(a)(ii) removes surcharge on section 196/197/198 income for a person whose income is chargeable under section 202; 4% cess remains under section 3(15)–(16).

## Required after-tax engine behavior

- Maintain a ledger by instrument, acquisition date, units, acquisition cost, transaction charges, distributions/coupons, and sale/redemption date.
- At each tax-year boundary, calculate each lot’s holding period and legal classification, aggregate eligible equity LTCG against the ₹1.25L allowance, calculate ordinary-income slabs/rebate/surcharge/marginal relief, and apply cess. Avoid treating withholding as final tax.
- Compare at least these exit cases: liquidate all lots in the target year; liquidate in tranches across tax years; hold a direct bond to contractual maturity while receiving coupons; and the applicable debt-fund redemption case. Do not model redemption proceeds and a coupon as the same cashflow.
- Reconcile terminal nominal balance to tax payable, fees and trading friction, then deflate using the selected observed CPI anchor and scenario paths. Keep CPI uncertainty separate from tax-law uncertainty.
- Before final use, validate against a qualified Indian tax professional’s worked examples for resident status, regime election, dividend/coupon timing, section 76 and Schedule IV eligibility.
